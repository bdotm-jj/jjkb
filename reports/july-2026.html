Key Findings & Recommendations

  1. Overall testing efficiency improved. Avg total duration per project dropped 19.5% vs. the Nov 2025 – Mar 2026 baseline (8.00d → 6.44d), even though R1 duration was identical in both periods (4.78d) — the improvement is concentrated in retest/regression rounds rather than initial testing effort.
  2. Retest compression continued. The R1→R2 reduction improved slightly from -44.2% (baseline) to -45.6% (current), showing retest cycles keep shrinking relative to first-round testing.
  3. Complex integrations still take the longest. Scottsdale API (15 days: 10d R1 + 5d retest) is the current period's longest project, comparable to baseline's longest (RenRe, 16 days) — large API/integration projects remain the biggest time investment.
  4. Several projects are mid-cycle. Auto Renewals – PL (13-day R1) and Markel Capacity Program, HVH GLISE Contract Phase 1, and GLISE Forms API have not yet logged retest rounds; their totals will likely grow in next month's report.
  5. Volume is lighter but steady. Project count held at 9 in both periods, but current-period task volume (15) is lower than baseline (23) — largely a function of several projects still awaiting R2/R3 rather than a true drop in testing activity.

Recommendations

  1. Keep tracking R1→R2 % reduction as the primary throughput metric — it's the most responsive indicator of process improvement and continues trending favorably.
  2. Flag Auto Renewals – PL and Scottsdale API for closer monitoring given their above-average totals; confirm whether length is driven by defect volume or scope size.
  3. Revisit this comparison once Chubb Local Rating, Munich RE, and the mid-cycle projects log additional rounds, so next month's totals reflect complete testing cycles.
