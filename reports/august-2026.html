Key Findings & Recommendations

Findings vs Nov 2025 – Mar 2026 Baseline

  • Total duration is up 11%. Avg project total rose from 8.0 to 8.9 days, with the increase concentrated almost entirely in R1.
  • R1 duration up 32% (4.8d → 6.3d) while R2 grew a more modest 12% (2.7d → 3.0d) — first‑round testing is taking meaningfully longer even as retests hold roughly steady.
  • R1→R2 reduction steepened from 44.2% to 52.4% — once a project reaches a second round, testing tightens up faster than in the baseline period, suggesting the bottleneck is concentrated at R1 rather than in the retest process.
  • Workflow Dashboard is a clear outlier at 25 total days (20‑day R1) — nearly 3x the current‑period average and 56% above the prior baseline high (RenRe, 16 days).
  • Fewer but larger engagements: projects tested rose from 9 to 10, while total tasks fell from 23 to 18 — this period's testing load is concentrated in fewer, longer efforts rather than many short ones.

Recommendations

1. Investigate R1 scope/defect drivers. Review Workflow Dashboard, Scottsdale API, and Auto Renewals - PL to determine whether expanded scope, higher defect volume, or resourcing gaps are extending first‑round testing.
2. Track pending retests. Auto Renewals - PL and Markel Capacity Program have logged only one round each — confirm whether additional rounds are planned, as their totals are likely understated.
3. Protect R2 retest discipline. R2 durations remain close to baseline; use current retest practices as the model while addressing R1 inflation rather than adding process overhead to the retest stage.
